# Parkinson's care checkpoints

Use these prompts only when relevant. They guide record review and clinician questions, not diagnosis or a treatment protocol. Verify current guidance and local services when using them.

## Diagnosis and function

Preserve the clinician's distinction between Parkinson's disease and parkinsonism. Diagnosis depends on clinical assessment; do not treat a scan, skin biopsy, alpha-synuclein assay, or genetic result alone as proof of a specific disorder. Ask what a proposed test would change. Record family history or genetic results only when supplied; consider counseling and consent before suggesting genetic testing. Use [NINDS](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/health-information/disorders/parkinsons-disease) for background and the specialist's assessment for the individual diagnosis.

Capture the person's priority: walking safely, working, sleeping, communicating, eating, independence, or care-partner support. Include motor symptoms and meaningful fluctuations. Record clinician-assessed MDS-UPDRS or Hoehn and Yahr stage only with documented context; neither defines a personal prognosis.

## Medication and hospital preparation

Preserve exact prescribed clock times and immediate/controlled/extended-release formulations. Do not convert doses, recommend catch-up doses, or suggest abruptly stopping antiparkinsonian medication. Discuss wearing off, dyskinesia, hallucinations, sleep attacks, or impulse-control changes with the prescriber. For unsafe sleepiness, prioritize driving safety and prompt care-team advice. See [NICE NG71](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng71/chapter/recommendations).

For admission or a procedure, prepare an accurate medication list, contacts, and a question about maintaining the individual schedule. Request pharmacist/neurology review of drugs that may worsen Parkinson's and an individualized plan if swallowing or fasting prevents the usual route. Do not advise crushing tablets, a new route, or taking medication against hospital instructions. Use the [Parkinson's Foundation Hospital Safety Guide](https://www.parkinson.org/resources-support/hospital-safety-guide) and current clinician resources; formulations and alternatives require professional review.

## Symptoms beyond movement

When relevant, ask about sleep, constipation, urinary difficulties, dizziness on standing, pain, fatigue, mood, thinking, hallucinations, and medication-related behavioral changes. These affect quality of life and need assessment rather than dismissal as inevitable. See [Parkinson's Foundation non-movement symptoms](https://www.parkinson.org/understanding-parkinsons/non-movement-symptoms).

For dizziness or fainting, record timing, falls, and existing blood-pressure observations without instructing an unsafe standing test. Do not prescribe extra salt or fluid; other conditions affect safety. See [Parkinson's Foundation dizziness and low blood pressure](https://www.parkinson.org/understanding-parkinsons/non-movement-symptoms/vertigo).

Ask about falls/freezing, speech, coughing with meals, swallowing medication, and weight change when relevant. Suggest clinician assessment and Parkinson's-experienced PT, OT, or speech-language therapy as appropriate. Do not invent a swallow-safe diet, swallowing maneuver, or exercise prescription. See [NICE rehabilitation recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng71/chapter/recommendations#non-pharmacological-management-of-motor-and-non-motor-symptoms) and [Parkinson's UK swallowing guidance](https://www.parkinsons.org.uk/professionals/resources/screening-swallowing-problems-parkinsons-nurse-charge-and-ward-staff-guide).

## Urgent changes

Put the action first and follow existing emergency/care-team instructions. Do not wait to finish a brief. If location is unknown, say local emergency services rather than inventing a number.

- **Emergency evaluation now:** Examples include stroke-like symptoms, severe breathing difficulty or choking with inability to breathe, unresponsiveness, serious injury, or fever with marked rigidity and confusion after interrupted Parkinson's medication. In hospital, alert the nurse/doctor immediately; outside, call local emergency services. Do not diagnose the cause or suggest a home medication rescue.
- **Prompt same-day clinical advice:** New confusion, distressing hallucinations, sudden worsening, repeated falls, inability to take prescribed medication, or new swallowing difficulty warrants prompt treating-team/urgent-care contact; escalate immediately if severe, worsening, or unsafe. Do not assume ordinary progression. The [Hospital Safety Guide](https://www.parkinson.org/resources-support/hospital-safety-guide) covers interrupted medication, swallowing, and hospital complications.
- **Planned review:** Stable ongoing symptoms and appointment preparation can enter the brief and next-visit questions. Existing clinician instructions or worsening symptoms take priority.

These are not exhaustive triage rules. Ask a minimal question only if it changes immediate action without delaying care.

## Research and source selection

1. Use local official guidance and national regulator labels for care and approved indications: for example [NICE NG71](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng71), [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs), Health Canada, EMA, MHRA, or TGA as appropriate. A US approval does not establish approval elsewhere.
2. Use [NINDS](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/health-information/disorders/parkinsons-disease), [Parkinson's Foundation](https://www.parkinson.org/), and [Parkinson's UK](https://www.parkinsons.org.uk/) for education/navigation. Identify nonprofit resources as such, not regulator guidance.
3. Use [ClinicalTrials.gov API v2](https://clinicaltrials.gov/data-api/api) and individual records for registration and sites. Registry listing does not establish efficacy or approval. Show record recency and confirm site access; absence from the helper does not prove no trials exist.
4. Use peer-reviewed primary studies for emerging questions, naming population, design, limitations, and whether findings concern symptoms or progression. Do not equate animal results, biomarker changes, or uncontrolled studies with demonstrated patient benefit.
5. Use official specialist-center pages for their own services and verified support organizations for practical help. Check geography, eligibility, referrals, and costs. Do not invent access or affiliation.

Do not use unsourced summaries, promotional clinic claims, social posts, or snippets as evidence. If a source is unavailable, report the limit rather than fabricate citations. Recheck these links and guidance when using the skill; this file was adapted on 2026-11-14.
